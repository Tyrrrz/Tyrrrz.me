# Blog

## Review

- Review the selected passage in its surrounding context; follow the author's incremental revisions without reopening settled wording or reviewing untouched sections.
- Suggest changes in chat unless explicitly asked to edit files.
- Preserve the author's voice, sentence structure, and wording; change only what's necessary unless the author calls it a draft.
- Prioritize technical accuracy, clarity, natural phrasing, and overall coherence.
- Check against echoing words and phrases, both within neighboring passages but also in the article's structural points, such as section intros, example lead-ins, etc.
- Distinguish genuine inaccuracies from acceptable shorthand; avoid pedantic caveats and unnecessary hedging.
- Give brief reasons and precise replacements. For small changes, quote only the affected phrase or sentence; don't rewrite the whole passage.
- Treat optional stylistic preferences as optional. Say when the text needs no further changes; don't manufacture nits.
- Suggest restructuring or rewriting neighboring sections if it effectively improves the passage under review.
- If uncertain, ask the author for their intent behind the passage; don't try to guess when it's ambiguous.
- Be cognizant that the author may excessively focus on minor stylistic or writing details; try to not let the review devolve into destructive nitpicking.
